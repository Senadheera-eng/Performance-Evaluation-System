# Attendance anomaly detection: evaluation

Generated by `ml/attendance_anomaly.py` on 2026-11-03. Trained on 8,405 simulated sign-ins; the flag threshold was set on 6,600 more; tested on 28,397 sign-ins in 720 lectures it never saw, 386 of them proxies.

Model deployed: **Isolation Forest (100 trees), scikit-learn 1.9.1**. A sign-in is flagged for review when its anomaly score is at least 0.643 (set so that about 1.5% of honest sign-ins in the validation lectures were flagged), or when it was made 1,000 m or more from the lecture: the forest ranks one far-off signal about as unusual as two mild ones together, and a sign-in kilometres away should always be seen.

| Approach | Proxies caught | Precision | Honest students flagged per lecture | Code relayed by photo | Friend's phone, switching accounts | Spare phone kept signed in | Their own phone, carried in | AUC |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Rules today (far or no location) | 41% | 3% | 6.69 | 94% | 9% | 9% | 13% | – |
| Every signal as a rule | 88% | 2% | 20.85 | 99% | 100% | 95% | 44% | – |
| Isolation Forest | 42% | 30% | 0.54 | 28% | 91% | 49% | 1% | 0.844 |
| Isolation Forest + 1 km rule | 47% | 32% | 0.54 | 40% | 91% | 49% | 1% | 0.844 |

## What the two newest signals add

A login to PES just before signing, and a sign-in from the same network and browser as another student's moments earlier, were added for a friend who signs themselves in and then switches to the absent student's account on their own phone. On the same test lectures, the model with only the first six signals caught 34% of those and 38% of all proxies, at 0.55 honest students per lecture; with all eight, 91% and 47%, at 0.54. Photo relays went from 51% to 40%: the same budget of false alarms is now shared with the new pattern.

Switching accounts in the same browser is not a matter for the model: the database refuses a second student from one browser in a lecture, records the attempt, and always lists the sign-in whose browser tried.

## Reading the numbers

- **Proxies caught** is the share of injected proxy sign-ins flagged; **precision** the share of flags that were proxies.
- **Honest students flagged per lecture** is the false alarms a lecturer would review in an average lecture.
- *Their own phone, carried in* leaves no trace in the data: the right phone, in the room, answering checks. No method here can catch it; it is in the test so the recall is honest.

Every number comes from a simulation (its assumptions are `SIM` in the script), because PES has almost no real live sign-ins yet. It shows the method works under those assumptions, not how often students cheat. Once a semester of real sign-ins exists, retrain with `--real` and check the flags against what lecturers find.
