# Attendance anomaly detection: evaluation

Generated by `ml/attendance_anomaly.py` on 2026-11-03. Trained on 7,447 simulated sign-ins; the flag threshold was set on 6,362 more; tested on 23,052 sign-ins in 720 lectures it never saw, 345 of them proxies.

Model deployed: **Isolation Forest (100 trees), scikit-learn 1.9.1**. A sign-in is flagged for review when its anomaly score is at least 0.626 (set so that about 1.5% of honest sign-ins in the validation lectures were flagged), or when it was made 1,000 m or more from the lecture: the forest ranks one far-off signal about as unusual as two mild ones together, and a sign-in kilometres away should always be seen.

| Approach | Proxies caught | Precision | Honest students flagged per lecture | Code relayed by photo | Second browser or phone | Their own phone, carried in | AUC |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Rules today (far or no location) | 44% | 4% | 5.85 | 95% | 12% | 11% | – |
| Every signal as a rule | 80% | 3% | 11.65 | 100% | 93% | 27% | – |
| Isolation Forest | 39% | 28% | 0.49 | 53% | 48% | 2% | 0.822 |
| Isolation Forest + 1 km rule | 41% | 29% | 0.49 | 56% | 48% | 2% | 0.822 |

## Reading the numbers

- **Proxies caught** is the share of injected proxy sign-ins flagged; **precision** the share of flags that were proxies.
- **Honest students flagged per lecture** is the false alarms a lecturer would review in an average lecture.
- *Their own phone, carried in* leaves no trace in the data: the right phone, in the room, answering checks. No method here can catch it; it is in the test so the recall is honest.

Every number comes from a simulation (its assumptions are `SIM` in the script), because PES has almost no real live sign-ins yet. It shows the method works under those assumptions, not how often students cheat. Once a semester of real sign-ins exists, retrain with `--real` and check the flags against what lecturers find.
