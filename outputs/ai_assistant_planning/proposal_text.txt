1. Introduction
The Performance Evaluation System (PES) is a comprehensive web-based platform designed to
transform how undergraduate students at the Faculty of Engineering, University of Sri
Jayewardenepura (USJ), interact with their academic journey. Currently, students face significant
challenges in tracking their academic performance, understanding attendance eligibility, selecting
appropriate courses and minors, and receiving timely academic guidance.
This project proposes the development of an intelligent academic management platform that
consolidates scattered academic information into a single unified system. The platform integrates an
AI-powered personal assistant that leverages machine learning algorithms to provide personalized
academic guidance, predict examination outcomes based on continuous assessment marks, and
proactively warn students about potential risks such as attendance non-eligibility.
Unlike existing student information systems that primarily serve administrative purposes, PES is
designed from the student's perspective. It empowers students with real-time insights into their
academic standing, provides intelligent course recommendations, enables digital medical certificate
submissions and examination enrollments, and creates a feedback loop between students and the
faculty administration.
The system also provides an administrative interface where faculty staff can publish examination
results, update attendance records, manage course information, and analyze student feedback—
creating a complete ecosystem for academic performance management.

2. Problem Statement & Objectives
The Faculty of Engineering at USJ currently lacks a centralized digital platform for students to
monitor their academic performance comprehensively. The following critical problems have been
identified through direct observation and student surveys:
 Attendance Unawareness
According to the Faculty Handbook (2023), students must maintain a minimum of 80% attendance in
each course to be eligible for the final examination. However, there is no real-time system for students
to track their attendance percentage. Students often realize they have fallen below the threshold only
after it is too late, resulting in course non-eligibility. Furthermore, students who miss lectures due to
medical reasons must submit medical certificates within 14 days (Faculty Handbook, Section 4.3), but
there is no automated reminder system for this critical deadline.
 Course Selection & Minor Confusion
The Faculty of Engineering offers minor specializations. For example Computer Engineering
department students have two minors High performance & Data management. Students must select
specific elective courses aligned with their chosen minor. However, no centralized system clearly
maps which courses belong to which minor, what prerequisites exist, or how credit requirements are
distributed across semesters. Students frequently make incorrect course selections, which can delay
their graduation or result in ineligibility for their desired minor.
 Scattered Academic Records
Academic records, examination results, course details, and timetables are currently dispersed across
multiple systems and communication channels. Results are often shared via notice boards or informal
channels. There is no single platform where students can view their complete academic history,
calculate their CGPA, or analyze their performance trends across semesters.
 Lack of Proactive Academic Guidance
Currently, there is no system that proactively warns students about potential academic risks. Students
rely on informal networks asking seniors or peers for guidance on course selection, examination
preparation, and understanding academic regulations. This informal information sharing often leads to
incomplete, outdated, or inaccurate advice, contributing to poor academic planning and preventable
failures.
 No Prediction or Early Warning Mechanism
Existing systems at Sri Lankan universities do not offer predictive analytics for student performance.
No mechanism exists to estimate a student's likely final grade based on mid-semester marks,
continuous assessment scores, and attendance patterns. Such predictive capabilities could enable early
intervention, allowing students and faculty to take corrective action before it is too late.

Aim
To design and develop a comprehensive, AI-integrated web-based platform that empowers
undergraduate students with real-time academic performance tracking, proactive guidance, and
intelligent insights, while providing faculty administration with efficient tools for academic
management.
Specific Objectives
1. Develop a centralized student dashboard displaying complete academic history, CGPA
calculations, and semester-wise performance trends.
2. Implement real-time attendance tracking with automated eligibility calculations and proactive
warning notifications when attendance drops below the 80% threshold.
3. Build an AI-powered conversational assistant using NLP and Decision Tree (CART)
algorithms that can answer academic queries, predict examination outcomes, and provide
personalized study recommendations.
4. Create a course and minor guidance module that helps students understand module
requirements, prerequisites, and elective options
5. Develop digital workflows for medical certificate submission, course enrollment, and
examination registration to replace existing manual processes.
6. Implement an anonymous and non-anonymous student feedback system to enhance teaching
quality and course content improvement.
7. Create an administrative panel enabling faculty staff to publish results, update attendance,
manage courses, and analyze student feedback.
8. Design the system with a modular, scalable architecture that can be adapted for other faculties
and universities beyond the initial deployment.

3. Literature Review & Existing Systems
This section reviews existing academic management systems both internationally and within Sri
Lanka, identifies their strengths and limitations, and establishes the research gap that PES aims to fill.
3.1 International Systems
 Degree Works (Ellucian)
Degree Works is a degree auditing and academic advising tool used in North American universities. It
tracks degree completion progress and provides 'what-if' scenarios for course selection (Ellucian,
2023). While it offers course planning capabilities, it does not provide AI-driven result predictions,
real-time attendance monitoring, or a conversational AI assistant.
 EduTrack – Student Performance Monitoring System
EduTrack is a commercial student performance monitoring system used in several international
universities. It provides GPA tracking, grade analytics, and basic performance dashboards for students
and administrators. However, EduTrack does not include AI-powered academic guidance, attendance-
based eligibility monitoring, or predictive analytics for examination results. It primarily functions as a
retrospective reporting tool rather than a proactive student support platform (Chen & Zhang, 2021).
 Blackboard Analytics for Learn
Blackboard Analytics provides institutional-level reporting and student performance dashboards. It
offers retention risk indicators and basic predictive models for identifying at-risk students. However,
it lacks a conversational AI assistant, does not provide course-specific result predictions based on
continuous assessments, and is not designed to handle institution-specific academic regulations such
as attendance eligibility thresholds (Blackboard Inc., 2024).
3.2 Sri Lankan Systems
 Performance Evaluation Service (PES) – Faculty of Applied Sciences, USJ
The Faculty of Applied Sciences at the University of Sri Jayewardenepura operates a Performance
Evaluation Service (PES) accessible at pes.science.sjp.ac.lk. This system allows students to log in and
view their semester results. Developed by Avomarks Solutions, it provides basic result viewing
functionalities and online course enrollment facilities. However, the system is limited to displaying
published results and does not offer real-time attendance tracking, AI-powered academic guidance,
predictive analytics, course selection guidance, or proactive warning systems. It functions primarily as
a result publication portal rather than a comprehensive academic performance management platform
(University of Sri Jayewardenepura, Faculty of Applied Sciences, 2024).
 University of Moratuwa – Student Information System
The University of Moratuwa operates a Student Information System (SIS) that manages student
registration, examination results, and basic academic records. However, this system primarily serves
administrative functions and does not provide students with real-time performance analytics, AI-
powered guidance, or proactive attendance warnings (University of Moratuwa, 2024).

4. Novelty & Unique Features
The novelty of the Performance Evaluation System extends far beyond simply targeting the Faculty of
Engineering students as its user base. PES introduces several innovative features that are not available
in any existing system used in Sri Lankan universities:
4.1 AI-Powered Conversational Academic Assistant
PES integrates a Natural Language Processing (NLP)-based AI assistant that students can interact
with conversationally. Unlike static FAQ pages or manual browsing, the AI assistant can:
• Answer complex academic queries in natural language (e.g., "What courses do I need for the
Data Management minor?")
• Access all system databases in real-time to provide personalized responses based on the
student's actual academic records
• Provide contextual guidance based on faculty regulations from the Faculty Handbook
• Generate motivational suggestions when a student's performance shows declining trends
4.2 Machine Learning-Based Result Prediction
PES implements a Decision Tree (CART) algorithm trained on historical student performance data to
predict final examination outcomes. This feature enables:
• Early identification of students at risk of failing based on mid-semester and CA marks
• Personalized study recommendations based on predicted weak areas
• Faculty-level analytics showing predicted pass/fail rates before examinations
4.3 Proactive Warning & Alert System
Unlike existing systems that are purely reactive (showing data only when requested), PES actively
monitors student metrics and generates warnings:
• Attendance Alerts: Automatic warnings when attendance drops below 85% (pre-warning)
and 80% (critical)
• Medical Certificate Reminders: 14-day deadline tracking with automated reminders
• Performance Decline Alerts: Notifications when GPA shows significant drops between
semesters
• Enrollment Deadlines: Reminders for course enrollment and exam registration periods
4.4 Faculty-Specific Regulation Engine
PES encodes the specific academic regulations of the Faculty of Engineering (from the Faculty
Handbook) into its business logic. This includes the 80% attendance rule, GPA calculation
methodology (credit-weighted), minor specialization requirements, examination re-sit rules, and
grading scales. This makes the system deeply integrated with the actual academic framework rather
than being a generic tool.

5.1 Data Privacy & Ethics
All student data will be handled in compliance with data protection principles. Personal information
will be encrypted at rest and in transit. Role-based access control (RBAC) ensures that students can
only view their own records. Administrative access is logged and auditable. Anonymous feedback
ensures student privacy when providing course evaluations. Explicit consent will be obtained for any
data used in AI model training.

6. System Architecture
The Performance Evaluation System follows a three-tier architecture pattern consisting of the
Presentation Layer (Frontend), Application Layer (Backend & AI), and Data Layer (Databases). The
system supports two primary user roles Students and Administrators each with distinct functionalities.
6.1 Architecture Description
Presentation Layer: Build with React.js and TypeScript, the frontend provides responsive, role-
based user interfaces. Students access their personalized dashboard, academic records, attendance data,
AI assistant, and enrollment modules. Administrators access the management panel for publishing
results, updating attendance, and managing courses.
Application Layer: The backend, build with Node.js and Express.js, handles authentication (JWT-
based), REST API endpoints, business logic processing, and the proactive warning engine. Supabase
Edge Functions are using for serverless processing of specific tasks.
AI/ML Layer: A dedicated Python-based AI module processes natural language queries from
students and runs the CART Decision Tree algorithm for result predictions. The AI module has read
access to all databases to provide contextual, personalized responses.
Data Layer: PostgreSQL (via Supabase) stores all structured data including student records, course
results, attendance records, course information, lecturer details, and feedback data. Row-Level
Security (RLS) policies ensure data access is restricted based on user roles.

7. AI Model – Training & Accuracy
The AI component of PES serves two primary functions: (1) a conversational assistant for answering
academic queries, and (2) a predictive model for forecasting examination results. This section
describes the approach for model training, feature selection, and accuracy evaluation.
7.1 Conversational AI Assistant
The conversational assistant utilizes a pre-trained Large Language Model (LLM) fine-tuned with
faculty-specific knowledge. The system employs Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) to combine
the LLM's language capabilities with real-time data retrieval from the PES databases.
Training approach:
• Knowledge Base Construction: The Faculty Handbook, course catalogs, academic
regulations, examination rules, and FAQ documents are processed and stored as vector
embeddings.
• RAG Pipeline: When a student asks a question, the system retrieves relevant documents from
the knowledge base, combines them with the student's academic context (from the database),
and generates a contextual response.
• Continuous Learning: Student interactions are logged (with consent) to identify common
queries and improve response quality over time.
7.2 Result Prediction Model
The result prediction model uses a Classification and Regression Trees (CART) algorithm, chosen
for its interpretability and effectiveness with structured academic data.

 Training Process
• Data Collection: Historical academic records from the past 3-5 years (approximately 500-
1000 student records per course) will be collected from the Faculty Examination Branch with
official permission.
• Data Preprocessing: Missing values will be handled using mean imputation for numeric
features and mode imputation for categorical features. Data will be normalized using Min-
Max scaling.
• Train-Test Split: The dataset will be split into 80% training data and 20% testing data using
stratified sampling to maintain class distribution.
• Model Training: The CART algorithm will be trained with hyperparameter tuning (max depth,
min samples split, min samples leaf) using 5-fold cross-validation.
• Output: The model predicts the student's likely grade category: A+/A, A-/B+, B/B-, C+/C, C-
/D+, or F.
 Accuracy Evaluation
The model's accuracy will be measured using the following metrics:
• Overall Accuracy: Percentage of correctly predicted grades across all categories.
Target: >=75%.
• Precision: For each grade category, the proportion of correct predictions out of all predictions
for that category.
• Recall: For each grade category, the proportion of correctly identified instances out of all
actual instances.
• F1-Score: Harmonic mean of precision and recall, providing a balanced measure.
Target: >=0.70.
• Confusion Matrix: A detailed matrix showing the distribution of predictions vs. actual
outcomes for each grade category.
• Cross-Validation Score: 5-fold cross-validation mean accuracy to ensure the model
generalizes well and is not overfitting.
 Model Improvement Strategy
If the initial CART model does not achieve the target accuracy, the following strategies will be
employed:
• Ensemble Methods: Random Forest or Gradient Boosting to combine multiple decision trees
for improved accuracy.
• Feature Engineering: Creating derived features such as 'improvement trend' (change in
marks across assignments) and 'peer comparison' (performance relative to batch average).
• Hyperparameter Optimization: Grid search or Bayesian optimization for finding optimal
tree parameters.
• Incremental Learning: Retraining the model each semester with new data to improve
predictions over time.

10. Expected Outcomes
Upon successful completion, the Performance Evaluation System is expected to deliver the
following outcomes:
10.1 Deliverables
1. Fully Functional Web Application: A production-ready, responsive web platform accessible
to students and administrators of the Faculty of Engineering, USJ, with secure authentication
and role-based access control.
2. AI-Powered Academic Assistant: A conversational AI chatbot capable of answering
academic queries, providing course guidance, and offering personalized study
recommendations using RAG and real-time database access.
3. ML-Based Result Prediction Model: A trained CART decision tree model achieving ≥75%
overall accuracy in predicting student grade categories based on continuous assessment and
attendance data.
4. Proactive Alert System: An automated warning engine that monitors student attendance and
performance metrics, generating timely notifications for attendance non-eligibility risks,
medical certificate deadlines, and performance declines.
5. Administrative Dashboard: A comprehensive management panel for faculty administrators
to publish results, update attendance, manage courses and lecturers, and view aggregated
analytics.
6. Digital Workflow System: Online modules for medical certificate submission, course
enrollment, examination registration, and student feedback replacing existing manual paper-
based processes.
10.2 Expected Benefits
1. Improved Student Awareness: Students will have real-time visibility into their attendance
percentages, CGPA, and academic standing, enabling informed decision-making throughout
the semester.
2. Reduced Academic Failures: The proactive warning system and AI-driven predictions are
expected to reduce preventable course failures by alerting students early about attendance and
performance risks.
3. Enhanced Administrative Efficiency: Digital workflows will reduce the time and effort
required for result publication, attendance management, and medical certificate processing by
an estimated 60-70%.
4. Data-Driven Decision Making: Faculty administrators will gain access to aggregated
analytics and performance trends, enabling evidence-based improvements in teaching and
curriculum design.
5. Scalable Model for Sri Lankan Universities: The modular architecture and configurable
regulation engine will serve as a template that can be adapted for other faculties and
universities across Sri Lanka.

11. Conclusion
This proposal presents the design and development plan for the Performance Evaluation System
(PES), an intelligent academic management platform tailored for the Faculty of Engineering at the
University of Sri Jayewardenepura. The system addresses critical gaps in the current academic
management infrastructure, where students lack centralized access to their academic records, real-time
attendance tracking, proactive academic guidance, and predictive performance insights.
Through the integration of AI-powered conversational assistance using Retrieval-Augmented
Generation (RAG) and machine learning-based result prediction using the CART algorithm, PES goes
beyond conventional student information systems. It transforms academic data into actionable insights,
enabling students to make informed decisions about their academic journey while providing faculty
administrators with efficient digital tools for academic management.
The literature review demonstrates that no existing system either internationally or within Sri Lanka
provides the comprehensive combination of features that PES offers: real-time attendance monitoring
with eligibility warnings, AI-powered academic guidance, machine learning predictions, faculty-
specific regulation handling, and integrated digital workflows, all within a single unified platform.
The feasibility of this project is supported by the well-defined technology stack (React.js, Node.js,
PostgreSQL, Python, Scikit-learn), the availability of required datasets through official faculty
channels, and a structured 12-month implementation timeline with clear milestones and deliverables.
The modular architecture ensures that the system can be extended and adapted for other faculties and
universities, maximizing its long-term impact.
In summary, PES represents a significant step forward in leveraging data management and artificial
intelligence to improve the undergraduate academic experience in Sri Lankan universities. By
addressing the identified problems with innovative, student-centric solutions, this project has the
potential to serve as a model for academic performance management systems across the higher
education sector.
